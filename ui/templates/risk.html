🧠 Unified Device Risk Intelligence

Hospital × device-category risk rows assembled from CMS hospital data, FDA MAUDE, device recalls, ClinicalTrials.gov, and CMS Open Payments. Each row is a single "risk hypothesis" — what the public data tells us about a specific device family at a specific hospital.

How to read this page in 30 seconds. Every row below answers one question: "For hospital X, for device family Y, what's the evidence of harm?" Because the US government publishes hospital data (CMS) and device-safety data (FDA) in separate silos with no shared ID, we can't say "this exact adverse event happened at this exact hospital." So every row is an indirect inference joined through an HCPCS↔FDA-product-code bridge — that's why every row carries confidence fields. Score ranges 0–10: 0–4 low 4–7 medium ≥7 high. How final_score is computed

Transparent, auditable formula — no ML here, just weighted counts + recall severity.

Step 1 — MAUDE severity

Weighted adverse-event count, log-scaled so 10 events ≈ 2.3, 100 ≈ 4.6, 1000 ≈ 6.9. 

weighted = deaths×3 + injuries×2 + malfunctions×1
maude_score = log(1 + weighted) × 2.3  (clamped 0–10)
Step 2 — Recall floor

If the device family has a recall, a floor kicks in — even with zero MAUDE events. 

Class I  → floor 9.0  (serious injury / death)
Class II → floor 6.5  (temporary harm)
Class III→ floor 4.0  (unlikely harm)

final_score = min(10, max(maude_score, recall_floor))  →  this is why you'll see score 9.00 even on categories with very few MAUDE events — a single Class I recall pins the floor up there.

⚠️ Important caveat — "hospital-device link" confidence is weak. MAUDE rarely names a specific hospital, and FDA recalls apply nationally, so every hospital that bills for this device inherits the same device-family risk score. The score describes the device, not whether this particular hospital is worse than its peers. To get hospital-specific risk you'd need a MAUDE-facility → CCN matcher (partial coverage at best) and/or CMS Medicare procedure volume for denominator normalization (rate = events per 1000 procedures).
